# Pattern audit v2: assess purpose, not a blacklist

## Decision order
1. Protect facts and recognizable voice. 2. Locate pattern at phrase / sentence / paragraph / post / series levels. 3. Judge whether it serves the actual reader. 4. Rewrite only where the pattern is needless. 5. Recheck intended meaning.

## Severity scale
- CRITICAL (hard gate): invented quote, result, experience, source; change of claim strength or causal direction; fabricated audience reaction; wrong attribution of client work as own product work.
- HIGH (edit on one clear occurrence): fake contrast where X was not disputed; empty standalone closing punch line; dramatic opener without content; invented expert consensus; unsupported market generalization; engagement bait; too-clever universal aphorism; promotional pivot unrelated to insight.
- MEDIUM (edit on clustering): exactly 3 rhythmic examples for aesthetic symmetry; neat four-act formula across multiple posts; repeated open-question ending; staged thesis after every anecdote; parallel line length; excess bold headings or one-sentence paragraphs; crutch transitions.
- LOW (usually preserve): common words (`important`, `however`, `actually`), appropriate passive voice, legitimate lists of 3, a single meaningful rhetorical question, a relevant em dash in someone else's quote. Author deliverable still follows no-em-dash preference outside verbatim quotes.

## Structural tests
A. FALSE CONTRAST: Could a real reader reasonably believe X? If yes, keep contrast. Otherwise state Y.
B. PARAGRAPH MIC-DROP: Does the closer add a testable fact, qualification or new question? If not, remove/merge.
C. SETUP/PAYOFF: Does a teaser delay the actual observation? Bring the observation forward.
D. TRIAD: Are all three items different and necessary? Keep if yes; do not delete real data to achieve two.
E. INANIMATE AGENCY: Is a metaphor useful and precise? Keep normal technical usage (`data suggests`); remove grandiose agency (`the market whispers`).
F. PASSIVES/ADVERBS: Never ban universally; leave when actor unknown, legally relevant, or emphasis genuinely needed.
G. FORMATTING: Does a heading orient a long article? Keep. Does it repeat the next line? Fix.
H. RHYTHM: Detect 3+ matching opening clauses, 3 consecutive similar-length sentences, line-break-per-phrase, identical endings; variation is not an end in itself.
I. POSITIONING: No prestige labels (`As a founder...`) unless needed to explain perspective.
J. EPISTEMIC PRECISION: Distinguish observed, measured, reported, inferred and hypothesized. Never hedge confirmed facts to appear modest or upgrade a single observation to a trend.
K. CULTURAL FRAME: Korea/global contrasts require clearly bounded cases; do not infer behavior of an entire population from a platform anecdote.
L. SERIAL VOICE: Across 3 to 6 posts compare opening syntax, personal-experience framing, paragraph breaks, last-line question, motif and CTA. Flag recurrence, not authenticity.

## Common false positives
`I noticed` can be a real first-person observation; `but` may make a real distinction; a list of 3 can report exactly 3 findings; a short sentence can contain a powerful concrete fact; and an em dash can be in an immutable source quote. No 'AI detection' claims.

## Phrase watchlist (contextual, not exhaustive)
`Ultimately`, `in today's rapidly evolving`, `the key takeaway`, `game changer`, `unlocks`, `seamlessly`, `delve`, `landscape`, `testament to`, `crucial`, `it is worth noting`, `here's the thing`, `let that sink in`, `this changes everything`, `not just X but Y`, `what struck me was`, `the real question is`, `I couldn't help but wonder`, `curious to hear your thoughts`. Check whether a real piece of information is hidden underneath before deleting.
