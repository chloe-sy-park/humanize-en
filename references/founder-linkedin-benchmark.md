# US startup founder LinkedIn writing benchmark (October 2026)

## Evidence and sampling
Qualitative review of publicly accessible LinkedIn posts, not a representative US-founder sample. Direct post examples include Alex Turnbull (bootstrapping and exit), Hendrix Liu (customer proximity and capital), and Miguel Moreira da Cruz (re-engagement scope). An additional 2026 industry analysis of 833 founder-category posts across 50 creators and 5,619 posts across 21 founder/CEO accounts offers directional quantitative context. The analyses are third-party, not independently audited; category does not imply all posters were US-based, and engagement does not prove authenticity or causality. Never say these traits describe all US founders. Benchmark English-speaking startup-founder communication, not US identity or accent.

## Observed features worth adapting (not copyable templates)
1. Situation before abstract advice: the reader can identify what happened, to whom, and under what constraint. Use project detail only when confirmed and shareable.
2. First person with a job: "I/we" describes an action, mistaken assumption, decision, or change, not a synthetic authority statement. Do not add 'I' when user only researched third-party data.
3. Tension has stakes: cost, missed step, surprising feedback, scope reduction, user response, or decision tradeoff. Do not invent numbers to imitate it.
4. Operational detail: show the interface step, campaign, customer route, resource constraint, or what changed after feedback; avoid sharing client-confidential data.
5. Specific claims, narrow inference: show the observation, then distinguish personal interpretation. Avoid converting one case into universal proof.
6. Human variation: some posts begin with a number, a task, a story, a direct statement, or a quote. Vary form across posts but never force numeric openers.
7. A useful ending can be an unresolved detail or question, but also a specific observation. No obligatory CTA.

## Observed but intentionally NOT adopted
- Aggressive numbers-first hook when numbers are absent.
- Broad universal prescriptions / hustle rhetoric / contrarian theatre.
- Staccato single-sentence paragraphs throughout, artificial rule-of-three, slogans, em dashes.
- "Here are the 5 lessons" machinery, relentless lessons, personal brand inflation.
- Synthetic vulnerability (invented failure, customer dialogue, hard-earned wisdom).
- Causal conclusions from engagement correlations.

## Diagnostic checks added to v3
A. Is the first 2-line preview specific enough to identify a person/action/constraint rather than merely naming a topic? Not always required but flag empty abstraction.
B. Does first-person language correspond to an actually performed act, or was it inferred from research?
C. Is the scope of each claim proportionate to its underlying sample/experience?
D. Is there an observed event or evidence, not a fictional founder-story container?
E. Does the post say anything intelligible without a manufactured punchline?
F. Is an industry term understandable to an AI/product founder without needless insider slang?
G. Check confidentiality, consent for quotes and identifiable client cases.
H. Across 3 posts: inspect identical hook types, paragraph length, 'I used to think/now' turns, final questions; suggest meaningful variation, never random novelty.
I. Native-speaker idioms are not a goal; concise natural business English is.

## Editorial grading add-on (not a second inflated score)
Keep original 50-point rubric. In addition give benchmark fit of LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH with concrete evidence, plus 'US founder voice overfit' risk of LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH. Any fabricated experience or citation is an automatic rejection independent of writing quality.

## Benchmarked openers: function, not phrases
- Concrete operating constraint -> why it changed a choice.
- Unexpected user reaction -> what was reconsidered.
- Distinct Korea vs global product observation -> scope and limits.
- Simple work diary -> open question still being worked through.
No sentence copying from sources. Never add an American identity, US company role or persona.
